# Requirements Matrix: 100 Points Explained

This matrix groups the 100 requirements into logical sections and communicates them in plain business language.

## 1. Academic Foundations and Vision
### 1. Define the university system vision and success criteria
The university must agree on goals, users, scope, and measurable success.

### 2. Define Maya as the primary teaching and tutoring platform
AI teaching support must be governed by academic policy, faculty oversight, and approved content use.

### 3. Map the full student lifecycle
From inquiry to graduation to alumni support.

### 4. Define learning models
Online, hybrid, on-campus, and self-paced structures.

### 5. Define institutional structure and units
Campus, faculty, department, and program mapping.

### 6. Define academic rules and progression
Prerequisites, credit hours, semesters, progression rules.

### 7. Define learning outcomes at program, course, and lecture levels
PLO, CLO, and LLO alignment.

### 8. Create central curriculum and course databases
Master data and approved curriculum repository.

### 9. Build course approval and version control
Governed changes and version tracking.

### 10. Integrate Maya with approved content
AI must use approved teaching materials only.

---

## 2. Teaching, Learning, Assessment, and Advising
### 11–16. Teaching delivery and attendance
Support live teaching, classroom engagement, online learning, attendance tracking, and time monitoring.

### 17–28. Assessment and academic support
Assignments, formative tests, grading, competency mapping, learning analytics, student dashboards, faculty dashboards, and advising systems.

---

## 3. Admissions, Student Administration, and Career Pathways
### 29–38. Admission and student lifecycle operations
Application processing, applicant CRM, AI counseling, registration, fee management, scholarships, internships, portfolios, and credentials.

---

## 4. People, Research, Campus Services, and Institutional Workflows
### 39–51. Faculty, staff, research, and service systems
Recruitment, payroll, research projects, library, labs, hostel, counseling, grievances, exam scheduling, graduation, alumni, and institutional workflows.

---

## 5. Access, Privacy, Security, and Compliance
### 52–61. Security and governance
RBAC, identity management, data privacy, retention, cyber monitoring, AI content validation, academic integrity controls, audit trails, and HEC compliance.

---

## 6. Management Information and Performance
### 62–64. Dashboards and KPIs
Leadership dashboards, dean and HOD dashboards, and institution-wide KPIs.

---

## 7. Connected Systems, Data, and Technology Platform
### 65–74. Platform foundation
APIs, master data management, web/mobile apps, cloud infrastructure, classrooms, low-bandwidth support, monitoring, and environment management.

---

## 8. Requirements, Design, and Controlled Development
### 75–80. Delivery method
Requirements writing, MVP prioritization, UX design, shared services, iterative releases, and integrated module development.

---

## 9. Verification, Pilot, Launch, and Support
### 81–90. Rollout and quality assurance
Testing, academic validation, pilot phased rollout, training, SOPs, data migration, support operations, and campus-by-campus launch.

---

## 10. Continuous Improvement, Responsible AI, and Scale
### 91–100. Long-term operating model
Continuous quality measurement, AI governance, predictive analytics, advisory AI, benchmarking, scaling to partner institutions, governance cycle, and product evolution.

---

## Summary
The 100 requirements collectively describe one integrated university platform with strong governance, secure data practices, AI oversight, academic integrity safeguards, and a phased roadmap for controlled transformation.
